KOSDAQ semiconductor etch consumables · Global monopoly in CVD SiC focus rings, a beneficiary of memory-etch consumables · Current price 201,500 KRW · Market cap ~2.25tn KRW (2026-07-22)

_Original 2026-06-21 (v7) · Figures updated 2026-07-22 (ref. price 201,500 KRW, Jul 22 close)_

- **Share price:** 201,500 KRW (52w range 90,400 – 348,500)
- **Market cap:** 2,252.7 bn KRW (~11.18M shares)
- **Trailing P/E:** 32.8 x (On FY25 NP of 69.9bn KRW)
- **Best-case TP (FY28E):** 206,355 KRW (+2.4% vs current price)

## 1. Company Overview

_Founding & business · earnings snapshot_

TCK manufactures Solid CVD SiC (silicon carbide) focus rings and wafers used in the semiconductor etch process. It was founded in 1996 as a joint venture of Japan's TOKAI CARBON, Korea's KC Ltd., and Germany's Schunk Carbon, and listed on KOSDAQ in 2001.

The company's essence is not a localization catch-up player but a global-monopoly consumables supplier. It holds an 80–90% world share of CVD Solid SiC focus rings — effectively a monopoly — with 6N (99.9999%) high purity and plasma-corrosion resistance as entry barriers.

It posted FY25 revenue of 301.3bn KRW and operating profit of 83.9bn KRW (27.8% OPM). Solid SiC accounts for 85% of revenue, and in 1Q26 that share rose to about 90%, deepening SiC concentration.

Focus rings are consumables that wear down and are replaced with each etch. Unlike equipment, which sells once at a capacity build-out, they generate recurring revenue in proportion to memory utilization (wafer input). Rising DRAM/NAND output at Samsung and SK hynix flows directly into replacement demand.

## 2. Ownership Structure

_Majority parent control · raw materials & dividends_

Japan's parent TOKAI CARBON holds 52.63%, a majority-control structure. Management control is stable, but with less than half the shares in free float, supply-demand volatility is high.

The company buys its key raw material — high-purity graphite — from its largest shareholder TOKAI CARBON. With the parent on the purchasing (cost) side, transfer-price adjustments act as a variable for the company's margin.

Its payout ratio has stayed above 20% for more than ten years. Dividend per share rose from 1,200 KRW in FY23 to 1,430 KRW in FY25, but with a dividend yield around 0.5% it has a growth-stock character.

## 3. Industry Analysis

_3.1 SiC focus ring · 3.2 Market structure & competition · 3.3 Memory cycle & demand_

We examine product/technology principles (3.1) → market structure & competition (3.2) → the memory cycle & demand (3.3), in that order.

### 3.1. What Is a SiC Focus Ring

A focus ring is the round ring inside an etch tool that surrounds the wafer and gathers the plasma (ions) uniformly out to the edge. Because uniform etching preserves chip-edge yield, precision matters more as processes shrink.

The focus ring is a consumable continuously worn by strong plasma and must be replaced at regular intervals. The more wafers are put in, the more frequently it is swapped.

Legacy quartz and silicon rings show durability limits as miniaturization intensifies etch strength. CVD SiC rings, with thermal stability above 1,600℃ and superior plasma-corrosion resistance, last longer and shed fewer particles — becoming the standard replacing quartz/silicon in leading-edge processes.

If a quartz ring is a cotton sleeve that frays often, a CVD SiC ring is a leather sleeve that doesn't wear out. It is expensive but lasts long and sheds little debris, so adoption rises the more precise the process.

### 3.2. Market Structure & Competition

In the global CVD Solid SiC focus-ring market the company's share is 80–90% — effectively a monopoly. Growing defect-free to several-mm thickness by CVD is a high-difficulty process, and as a core etch part with high customer-qualification and switching costs, entry barriers are high.

There are cracks in the monopoly. Late entrants such as YMC, Wycom, and DS Techno entered the after-market (direct supply to chipmakers) and eroded share for a time; the company won a final victory in 2024 in a material-property patent suit filed in 2020 and is pushing to recover share.

Intensifying competition already shows in margins. OPM fell from about 39% in FY22 to 27.8% in FY25. A monopoly, yes, but with less pricing power than before.

### 3.3. The Memory Cycle & Demand

There are three paths to the company's revenue growth. Two of them work even without producing more memory.

First, the more Samsung and SK hynix run memory, the more rings are replaced. As consumables, ring demand follows new fab starts and rising utilization. Equipment sells once at a build-out, but rings keep selling as long as production runs.

Second, the number of etch steps per wafer is rising. As 3D NAND stacks to 400–1000 layers and DRAM circuits shrink, deep, difficult etches surge. The more etching, the more the ring wears, raising the number of rings consumed per wafer.

Third, SiC is replacing quartz/silicon rings. Even at the same volume, a higher SiC adoption share lifts the company's revenue.

The key point is that even with memory output flat, the latter two (etch steps · SiC substitution) alone can grow revenue — a structurally upward-sloping profile beyond a simple build-out beneficiary.

### 4.2. Before-market / After-market Structure & Share

The company is a before-market player that supplies rings to equipment makers (Lam, AMAT, TEL, Chinese OEMs); those rings then enter chipmakers loaded on the tools. The after-market — selling replacements directly to chipmakers — is the domain of rivals such as K&J, DS Techno, and Hana Materials.

Market sizes are back-calculated estimates from K&J's ~75bn KRW 2025 revenue and 60%+ after-market MS (DailyInvest) and the company's SiC revenue (254.8bn). The 20–30% price gap and 1–2yr lag are per Dealsite/TechWorld. Exact market size by channel is undisclosed.

The company's before-market MS is a solid 90%+. In the after-market K&J has risen to 60%+ MS with the company at a disadvantage, and expanding after-market-part adoption by chipmakers was the cause of the company's past revenue de-growth.

The current cycle favors the company. Leading-edge-process usage rises first, so before-market revenue leads, and after-market players follow with a 1–2 year lag. The Gen-9 NAND transition and DRAM expansion are reflected first in the company, a before-market supplier.

The source of its bargaining power is not that it is a Korean company but its CVD SiC monopoly technology. With multiple equipment-maker customers its single-customer dependence is low, and it is adopted because there is no alternative supplier. Still, after-market erosion has weakened the monopoly premium of the past.

## 5. Financial Analysis

_Last 5 years, consolidated_

**Growth:** revenue plunged from a FY22 peak of 334.4bn KRW to 226.7bn (−32%) in the FY23 downcycle, then recovered to 301.3bn in FY25 — a structure that swings ±30% with the memory cycle.

**Profitability:** OPM fell about 11pp from the ~39% range in FY22 to the ~28% range in FY25. The result of downcycle fixed-cost burden compounded by late-entrant competition, the monopoly margin stepped down a notch.

**Financial structure:** with FY25 assets of 567.6bn KRW and liabilities of 48.3bn, a ~9% debt ratio makes it a virtually debt-free net-cash company. With retained earnings of 507.6bn, its stamina to withstand a downcycle is excellent.

1Q26 net profit was 25.1bn KRW, +29% vs 19.5bn a year earlier — the opening phase reflecting a recovery in etch-consumable demand from DRAM expansion and rising NAND layer counts.

### 6.4. Risk Factors

- **Memory-cycle dependence:** tied to Samsung/SK utilization, with risk of a −30%-range plunge in a downcycle (FY23 −32%).
- **Late-entrant competition · margin pressure:** entry of YMC, Wycom, and DS Techno cut OPM from 39% in FY22 to 28% in FY25.
- **Single-segment concentration:** 85–90% of revenue is the single Solid SiC segment, so change in this area flows straight into company-wide earnings volatility.
- **Parent raw-material dependence:** the key raw material graphite is bought from the parent, so transfer pricing is a cost/margin variable.
- **Free-float constraint:** with the parent's 52.63% stake, less than half the shares are in free float, so supply-demand volatility is high.
- **Valuation:** at a current trailing P/E of about 33x, it has de-rated from June (47x). If growth slows further, downside risk remains.

## 7. Conclusion

_No.1 share · dependent on the leading-edge expansion cycle_

TCK is an etch-consumable supplier holding an 80–90% global share of CVD SiC focus rings. However, the recurring replacement demand proportional to Q growth flows to the after-market (K&J, etc.), so what the company actually captures is closer to leading-edge-process expansion (before-market).

Late-entrant competition brought OPM down from 39% to 28%, weakening pricing power. Limiting revenue growth to leading-edge expansion and fixing OPM at the current level, even in a super-cycle (Best) the target price of 206,355 KRW exceeds the current price by +2.4%.

In sum, it is indeed the No. 1 in share, but its pricing power has been trimmed, and the Q-proportional recurring revenue largely belongs to after-market rivals, so the company is heavily dependent on the leading-edge-expansion cycle. The current price has corrected from June to near the conservative-scenario band (Best +2.4%), but the structure of dependence on upside the company cannot capture remains, so conservatively viewed the entry appeal is limited.

NAND pure-play · assessing value at the AI supercycle peak · Tokyo Stock Exchange Prime · NAND flash / SSD · share price 63,290 JPY · market cap ~34.6tn JPY (2026-07-22)

_Original 2026-06-21 (v5) · Figures updated 2026-07-22 (ref. price 63,290 JPY, Jul 22 close)_

- **Trailing P/E:** 62 x (On FY25 EPS of 1,015 JPY)

## 1. Company Overview

_Founding history · #3 in global NAND · earnings snapshot_

Kioxia is a Japanese pure-play memory company that makes NAND flash memory and SSDs. It traces its roots to the Toshiba division that invented NAND flash — a world first — in 1987; it was spun off from Toshiba in 2018, renamed Kioxia in 2019, and listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange in December 2024.

It is the world's #3 NAND player after Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. A defining feature is its joint-venture structure, co-operating the Yokkaichi (Mie) and Kitakami (Iwate) production fabs with SanDisk (formerly Western Digital).

FY2025 (Apr 2025–Mar 2026) revenue was 23,376 (100M JPY), +37% YoY, and operating profit hit a record high of 8,700 (100M JPY). This came alongside surging AI-data-center NAND/SSD demand and a spike in average selling prices (ASP).

After standalone fiscal-4Q (Jan–Mar 2026) revenue of 10,029 (100M JPY) and operating profit of 5,968 (100M JPY), FY2026 1Q (Apr–Jun 2026) guidance is set at 17,500 (100M JPY) revenue and 13,000 (100M JPY) operating profit (74% OPM) — a phase in which the NAND cycle is climbing steeply toward its peak.

## 2. Ownership / Shareholders

_Bain carve-out structure · shareholder base_

The ownership is a carve-out structure formed when the Bain Capital consortium acquired Toshiba Memory in 2018. Since the December 2024 listing, Bain has been in an exit phase, gradually trimming its stake from the low-50%s to the mid-30%s.

Toshiba retains a domestic anchor-shareholder role at about 27%, and SK hynix holds about 14% including convertible bonds (CBs) but with voting rights capped at about 15% through 2028. The key watch point of the ownership is that Bain's further block deals / secondary offerings remain a supply/demand variable.

## 3. Industry Analysis — NAND Structure & the Supercycle

_3.1 How it works · 3.2 5-player oligopoly · 3.3 AI demand & supply bottleneck_

We proceed in order: how NAND flash works (basics) → the global oligopoly (market) → the cycle created by AI demand and supply bottlenecks (external variables).

### 3.1. How NAND Flash and SSDs Work

NAND flash is non-volatile memory that retains data even when power is off. It stores 0s and 1s by trapping electrons in cells; if DRAM is the temporary workspace on your desk, NAND is the permanent storage drawer.

3D stacking — building cells vertically — governs density. Today more than 200 layers are stacked to pack more capacity into the same area, and the number of layers plus process miniaturization are the core of cost competitiveness.

An SSD is a storage device made by attaching a controller to several of these NAND chips. In particular, enterprise SSDs for AI data centers demand high capacity and reliability, command high prices, and are Kioxia's core growth axis.

### 3.2. Global Competitive Landscape — 5-Player Oligopoly

NAND is an oligopoly in which the top five makers hold more than 90% of the market. Kioxia is 3rd at about 14% by revenue, but combined with JV partner SanDisk it commands roughly 30% on a bit basis — production capacity rivaling Samsung.

That said, Kioxia has a NAND single-business structure with no DRAM/HBM. Unlike SK hynix, Samsung, and Micron — which have a separate growth engine and margin cushion in HBM — its earnings are 100% tied to the NAND cycle, a structural differentiator and a volatility factor.

### 3.3. AI Demand & the Supply Bottleneck — Drivers of the Cycle

To start with the conclusion, the current NAND boom is a structural tightening in which surging AI-data-center demand meets supply that was suppressed for years. The 2026 NAND market is forecast to grow +112% YoY to 22tn JPY ($147B) (TrendForce, converted at ¥150/$; all market sizes below are unified in yen).

With the shortage expected to last into 2027–2028, the FY2028E market size has a wide scenario range. From a 2026 base of 22tn JPY, Base assumes normalization (~+8%/yr → ~25.5tn JPY), Bull continuation (~+33%/yr → ~39tn JPY), and Best a structural supercycle (~+65%/yr → ~60tn JPY).

The mechanism is clear. As generative-AI inference and training grow, enterprise-SSD orders explode, but because memory makers cut investment in the 2022–2024 downcycle, new capacity only comes online in late 2027–2028. Demand has outrun supply, and pricing power has passed entirely to the memory makers.

#### Memory's share of hyperscaler capex — outlook (source: SemiAnalysis · CLSA)

The structural shift in demand is also evident in hyperscaler investment allocation. Memory's share of total capex is forecast to jump from 7.7% in 2024 to 13.5% in 2025, 35% in 2026, and 48% in 2027 (SemiAnalysis · CLSA).

However, this share is on a total-memory basis including DRAM/HBM. The bulk of the increase is HBM/DRAM, and Kioxia captures the AI-server enterprise-SSD (NAND) portion of it. So the intensity of NAND's standalone benefit may be gentler than the total-memory curve — a point to watch alongside.

In 1Q, Kioxia's shipments actually fell about 10%, yet ASP doubled, sending revenue and profit soaring — a textbook supply-shortage phase where price overwhelms the volume decline.

On the risk side, NAND is inherently a cyclical commodity. A signal of AI-capex slowdown, or 2028 new capacity coming online, could quickly flip the shortage into a glut, in which case today's price premium rapidly evaporates.

## 4. Business Model / Customer Concentration

_4.1 Revenue mix · 4.2 Customers & end-demand_

### 4.1. Revenue Structure — A Single NAND/SSD Axis

Kioxia's revenue is effectively a single NAND/SSD business. Products split into enterprise SSDs for data centers and client SSDs/embedded memory for PCs and smartphones, with AI-oriented enterprise SSDs driving recent growth.

In FY2025, operating cash flow surged to 6,165 (100M JPY) and the equity ratio improved from 25.3% to 37.9%. It is a phase of rapidly cutting the heavy debt load from the carve-out era with boom-period cash.

### 4.2. Customers & End-Demand

End-demand is rapidly concentrating on AI data centers and cloud service providers (CSPs). It is broadening its enterprise-SSD references — for example, jointly launching a 9.8PB-class high-density storage server with Dell.

That said, its pricing power is less Kioxia's own strength than cyclical bargaining power arising from the whole industry's supply shortage. The structural limit of the business model is that once NAND turns to oversupply, prices and margins quickly revert.

## 5. Financial Analysis — Last 5 Years

_Cyclicality · profitability · financial structure_

Cyclicality: Revenue fell for two consecutive years in the downcycle, from 15,265 (100M JPY) in FY2021 down to 10,766 (100M JPY) in FY2023, then more than doubled on the FY2024–2025 AI boom. Operating profit swung from a −2,527 (100M JPY) loss in FY2023 to an 8,700 (100M JPY) profit in FY2025, showing the full amplitude of the memory cycle.

Profitability: OPM swung nearly 60 percentage points, from −23.5% in FY2023 to +37.2% in FY2025. This extreme volatility is the essence of a NAND single business and the reason margin must be treated as a scenario variable in valuation.

Financial structure: Using boom-period cash to cut debt, the equity ratio recovered to 37.9%. But carve-out debt and large expansion investments remain, so if the cycle turns, interest and depreciation burdens could weigh on margins again.

## 6. Valuation — 3 Scenarios

_6.1 Applied P/E · 6.2 Scenarios · 6.3 Narratives · 6.4 Risks_

At 63,290 JPY the current price is about 62x trailing P/E on FY2025 EPS of 1,015 JPY; but as the AI boom sends earnings soaring, the forward P/E on an FY2026 basis falls to roughly 8–9x. The key question is whether this peak profit persists and expands through FY2028.

Reflecting NAND's tendency to trade at a low P/E at the cycle peak, a single nerfed 8x P/E — below the global memory-peer average — is applied to all three scenarios. Revenue is derived top-down as global NAND market size (TAM, tn JPY) × Kioxia's share, with scenario differences arising only in the two variables of TAM and OPM. All amounts are unified in yen (market size converted at ¥150/$).

### 6.1. Applied P/E

The peer average is 7.8x, and SanDisk — the most direct comparable as a NAND pure-play — is being re-rated at 11.7x. Because Kioxia and SanDisk jointly operate the Yokkaichi and Kitakami fabs on effectively the same NAND production base, SanDisk is the core peer.

From a conservative view that accounts for the high cyclical volatility of a NAND single business but assumes no multiple expansion, 8x — close to the peer average (7.8x) and below Kioxia's own rolling (8.8x) and SanDisk (11.7x) — is applied commonly to all three scenarios.

### 6.3. Scenario Narratives

#### Base — Cycle normalization, run-rate maintained

A scenario in which the NAND market normalizes from its 2026 peak, with FY2028 TAM roughly flat at 25.5tn JPY ($170B, ~+8%/yr vs 2026). Assuming a 14% share gives revenue of 3.6tn JPY, with OPM normalizing to 35%. Operating profit 1.2tn JPY, net profit 0.9tn JPY, EPS 1,670 JPY — at an 8x P/E, a target price of 13,362 JPY (−78.9%).

#### Bull — Tightening persists, moderate growth

A scenario in which the supply shortage lasts into 2027–2028, expanding NAND TAM to 39tn JPY ($260B, ~+33%/yr vs 2026) while maintaining a 15% share and 48% OPM. Revenue 5.9tn JPY, net profit 2.0tn JPY, EPS 3,754 JPY — a target price of 30,030 JPY (−52.6%).

#### Best — Structural supercycle (Goldman thesis)

An upside scenario in which AI demand reshapes NAND into a structural growth market, surging FY2028 TAM to 60tn JPY ($400B, ~+65%/yr vs 2026) and reaching a 16% share and 58% OPM. Revenue 9.6tn JPY, net profit 4.1tn JPY, EPS 7,443 JPY — a target price of 59,544 JPY (−5.9%). This runs in the same direction as Goldman Sachs' bullish case, which sets a 93,000 JPY target on FY2028 operating profit of 10tn JPY, though this report keeps TAM conservative. The surge in memory's share of hyperscaler capex (48% forecast for 2027, SemiAnalysis) is the key basis for this structural-demand scenario.

The reason all three scenarios sit below the current price is clear. The current price embeds the premise that FY2026 peak-quarter profit (1Q net profit 0.87tn JPY) persists and expands through FY2028; but top-down, that level only holds in a structural supercycle where NAND TAM grows more than 3x versus 2026.

The conditions for an upgrade of this verdict are clear. If AI-inference SSD demand structurally lifts the memory cycle so that Kioxia's OPM is sustained in the 60%s, or a U.S. ADS listing confers a Micron/SanDisk-level valuation premium, above-Best is possible. Conversely, if an AI-capex slowdown coincides with 2028 capacity coming online, it is quickly downgraded below Base.

### 6.4. Risk Factors

- **Cycle-peak risk** — the current price pre-reflects multi-year persistence of FY2026–2027 peak earnings. If NAND swings to oversupply, prices/margins plunge and it could converge to the Base (−78.9%) scenario.
- **NAND single business** — with no separate margin cushion like DRAM/HBM, earnings are 100% tied to the NAND cycle. Volatility is structurally higher than peers.
- **Overhang** — Bain Capital's (~36%) phased exit and further secondary offerings / block deals remain a supply overhang.
- **Valuation volatility** — trailing P/E ~62x and PBR ~26x make multiples extreme. Analyst target prices span 17,000–93,000 JPY, a 5x+ spread, i.e., no consensus.
- **FX / capex burden** — yen/dollar moves directly affect the yen translation of dollar revenue. Carve-out debt and large expansion capex weigh on margins in a downcycle.

## 7. Conclusion

_Cycle peak · overvalued across the board on earnings_

On an earnings basis — Base −78.9% · Bull −52.6% · Best −5.9% — all three scenarios sit below the 63,290 JPY current price, so the benefit of new buying is limited.

Kioxia has a NAND single-business structure with no DRAM/HBM, so earnings are 100% tied to the NAND cycle, and the current price pre-reflects multi-year persistence of FY2026 peak-quarter profit (1Q net profit 0.87tn JPY).

To justify the current price at an 8x P/E would require a structural supercycle in which FY2028 NAND TAM grows to about 64tn JPY ($425B), roughly 2.9x versus 2026 — an extreme premise in which NAND ceases to be a cyclical commodity.

Holders should recognize this is a cyclical commodity whose price hinges on the debate over the durability of peak earnings, and reasonably use as confirming indicators the structural persistence of AI-inference SSD demand and Kioxia's OPM staying in the 60%s, a valuation re-rating from a U.S. ADS listing, and the clearing of Bain Capital's overhang.

1Company Overview

Basic information · business model · product mix

1.1 Basic Information

SPG Co., Ltd. is a Korean company that has built a leading position in small geared motors since its founding in 1991. Listed on KOSDAQ in 2002, it specializes in drive components used across industrial machinery, home appliances, and medical devices.

1.2 Core Business Model

SPG's revenue rests on a geared-motor solution: a "motor" that converts electrical energy into mechanical rotation, integrated with a "reducer" that adjusts speed to amplify torque. With a standard lineup of 3,500+ motor types, it serves diverse downstream industries, and it reinvests that stable cash flow into precision-reducer R&D for robotics — a virtuous cycle.

Most notably, in the precision-reducer market (SH strain-wave and SR/RV reducers) long monopolized by Japanese firms, SPG became the first Korean company to mass-produce a full lineup, accelerating its shift into a higher-value robot-parts supplier.

1.3 Product Mix

Traditional industrial and appliance motors still dominate revenue, but the contribution of precision reducers is rising sharply.

3Business Analysis (Key Driver)

Segment & downstream analysis · investment points & moat

3.1 Segment Status & Downstream Analysis

SPG's business splits into "traditional standard motors" and the "growth engine of precision reducers." The maturity of appliances/automation and the explosive growth of robotics are driving a portfolio shift.

3.1.1 Appliance & Industrial Automation Motors

Over 60% of revenue comes from appliance motors, with strength in small geared motors for refrigerator ice dispensers, air purifiers, and clothes dryers. Geography is balanced — Korea (36.0%), China (27.4%), Americas (27.1%) — with Samsung, LG, GE, and Whirlpool as anchor customers. Despite a low-growth appliance backdrop, replacement demand for high-efficiency BLDC motors and premium-appliance expansion support a steady 3–5% annual growth.

3.1.2 Robot Precision Reducers (Growth Driver)

The reducer market was historically split between Japan's Harmonic Drive Systems (HDS, small/harmonic) and Nabtesco (large/RV). From around 2020, SPG entered by localizing SH (strain-wave) and SR (RV) reducers. Precision-reducer revenue is estimated at ~13bn KRW in 2024, with the company targeting 30bn KRW in 2025 and 50bn+ KRW by 2027. UPDATED 2024 robot precision-reducer revenue has been confirmed to have crossed 10bn KRW, and the humanoid drive actuator (SDD) is developed with mass production from 1H 2026.

3.2 Investment Points & Technical Moat

3.2.1 The only maker able to mass-produce a full reducer lineup

SPG is the only company in the world able to produce all three — planetary, SH, and SR reducers. Unlike Japanese rivals concentrated in a single type (HDS-SH, Nabtesco-SR), SPG can supply every reducer type a robot maker needs as a total solution, raising design convenience and reducing procurement risk for customers.

3.2.2 Price & lead-time edge through localization

SPG's biggest strength in precision reducers is cost efficiency — a price advantage of ~40%+ versus Japanese product, and lead times cut from up to a year on imports to within one month via domestic production. This is a powerful draw for robot makers preparing for mass production, and the basis for order expansion after proof-of-concept (PoC).

3.2.3 Riding the 'Physical AI' and cobot wave

Robotics is evolving into "physical AI," spreading across manufacturing, logistics, and domestic use. Demand for SH reducers — essential to collaborative and humanoid robots — is surging. SPG supplies cobot and humanoid reducers via its Rainbow Robotics partnership and has landed in tier-1 supply chains such as Samsung's "BotFit" drive module.

⚠ UPDATE — Blackstone bought Futronic, a competitor, not SPG On Jul 20, 2026 Blackstone agreed to take a controlling stake (>50%) in Futronic (~$720M / ~1T KRW), a Korean automotive actuator / motion-control firm expanding into humanoids. This is often mis-attributed to SPG. Futronic is a direct peer, so read it as a two-sided event, not a direct SPG catalyst.
Positive read-through — sector validation
  • The world's largest PE firm paying ~1T KRW for a Korean actuator maker validates the physical-AI reducer/actuator thesis with real capital.
  • Private-market pricing anchors the floor: Futronic (single axis) ≈1T KRW vs SPG (full lineup, listed) ≈1.46T KRW — SPG's premium is not excessive.
  • Expect broader global-PE attention on Korean reducer / sensor / motion-control names.
Caution — intensifying competition
  • Futronic competes directly in actuators / humanoid drive modules, now armed with Blackstone's capital and network.
  • Localization competition shifts from SPG-alone to a well-capitalized multi-player race — monitor the durability of SPG's price/lead-time moat.

4Financial Projection

Updated for the current price and market cap

UPDATED The February note argued the stock was in overvaluation territory: a trailing P/E of ~252x and ~59x even on a 2029-forward basis. Since then the shares have fallen ~55% — from ~148,600 to 66,000 KRW — so the market cap in the model moves from 3.30T to 1.46T KRW and the trailing P/E normalizes to ~112–155x.

The projection keeps the original assumptions: precision-reducer volume of ~130k units in 2026 rising to ~330k by 2029; reducer-segment operating margin of 20% (up to 30% in the Bull case), with existing segments' margins rising gradually; and net profit taken as 70% of operating profit for taxes and other costs.

⚖ UPDATE — my model vs post-deal sell-side My conservative 25x model calls the current price fair (upside +0–6%). Post-deal, the sell-side is more bullish: IBK set a 100,000 KRW target ("humanoid reducer growth kicking in", +51%) and Hana flagged SPG as a physical-AI beneficiary, projecting 30bn+ KRW of pure-robot revenue (~7.2% of total) in 2026. The gap between the two is the whole question — and an actual mass-production order track record is what must close it.

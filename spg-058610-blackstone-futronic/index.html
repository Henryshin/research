1.2 What it means for SPG — both edges

Positive read-through — sector validation
  • The world's largest PE firm paying ~1T KRW for a Korean actuator maker validates the "physical AI → surging reducer/actuator demand" thesis with real capital.
  • Private-market pricing anchors the floor: Futronic (single axis) ≈1T KRW vs SPG (full motor+reducer lineup, listed) ≈1.46T KRW — SPG's premium is not excessive.
  • Expect broader global-PE attention on Korean reducer / sensor / motion-control names.
Caution — intensifying competition
  • Futronic is a direct competitor in actuators / humanoid drive modules, now armed with Blackstone's capital and network.
  • Localization competition shifts from SPG-alone to a well-capitalized multi-player race — monitor the durability of SPG's price/lead-time moat.
  • Community skepticism about robot-hardware hype is real and loud.

3Fundamentals Check

Moat intact, risks intensifying
Intact growth drivers
  • 2024 robot precision-reducer revenue crossed 10bn KRW.
  • Only Korean maker mass-producing all three — planetary, harmonic (SH), RV (SR). Core moat intact.
  • Humanoid drive actuator (SDD) developed; mass production from 1H 2026.
  • Supplies Rainbow Robotics (RBY1), Samsung "BotFit" drive module — anchored in tier-1 supply chains.
  • 1-month lead time vs Japanese peers' 6+ months.
Sharpened risks
  • Customer concentration: heavy reliance on names like Rainbow Robotics.
  • Theme volatility: robot names react to news before earnings — hence the –60% drawdown.
  • New competition: a Blackstone-backed Futronic could dilute the localization premium.
  • Execution risk: hitting reducer targets (30bn 2025 → 50bn+ 2027 KRW) drives the multiple.
Ownership / overhang: largest shareholder & related parties ~38.45% (stable control). No large unconverted CB/BW dilution outstanding — low overhang risk.
